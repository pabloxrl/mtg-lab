# Independent plan: AI-generated POC games and cross-engine replay

Status: planning only, 2026-09-27. No game execution, build, implementation, or qualification is claimed. Read independently from AGENTS.md, RFC 0001/0002, the frozen card manifest, the program task manifest, neutral-scenario/comparator documentation, and both committed smoke bridge sources/pins. No other planning drafts were consulted.

## Recommendation

Build a **generate once, freeze semantic choices and chance outcomes, replay three times** pipeline. AI supplies candidate decisions during generation; strict controllers execute the resulting immutable transcript in mtg-lab, XMage, and Forge. Do not ask each engine's own AI to play a supposedly equivalent game. Policies may disagree and their decisions depend on their own observations, candidate ordering, heuristics, random-number consumption, and rules implementation. A common seed neither aligns different RNG algorithms and shuffle implementations nor aligns later draws after different mulligans/decisions. Even identical RNG algorithms may consume numbers differently. Record actual shuffle permutations and decisions, rather than treating seeds as a cross-engine protocol.

Start with reviewed short replay prefixes and adapter negative controls, then complete normal-reset games. Large AI campaigns are valuable discovery tools, but do not replace rule-derived fixtures, the independent critical suite, or field-level checks. LLM-authored prose is not an executable game or a rules oracle. A native seeded random policy and a simple heuristic policy should generate the bulk; optional LLM agents propose coverage targets and choose explicit legal candidates through the same seat-filtered interface. Freeze their accepted actions so later replay needs no model service.

## Replay contract and adapter work

Extend the existing neutral-scenario contract deliberately, with versioned migration when needed. Do not overload the minimal Rust comparator v1. Reuse its strict parser/provenance principles and the full neutral checkpoint comparator; current 4 MiB input bounds may require a separately versioned streaming full-game envelope rather than silently relaxing existing validation.

A game artifact contains deck/seat pins, starting seat, complete initial ordered libraries, ordered mulligan reshuffle results, ordered semantic action/choice records, checkpoint requirements, generation provenance and final accounting. Chance events have sequence IDs, kind, actor, expected pre-event multiset and complete resulting permutation. Each adapter consumes exactly one matching event at the actual shuffle request; never reshuffle independently. An unexpected shuffle, missing choice, unused event, or extra callback fails. Verify hooks inject randomness only; they must not overwrite downstream state to force agreement. Initial injected orders need not match upstream RNG behavior; they must be valid permutations and pass through genuine normal reset and draw/mulligan rules.

Bind physical deck occurrences at creation, e.g. `(seat, card_id, copy_index)`, independently of mutable library position. Map actual engine IDs at insertion. Zone changes receive canonical generation identifiers, while lineage identifies the original physical card. Tokens use `(creation_action, ordinal)` plus generation. Pending triggers use source incarnation, ability identifier and triggering event; stack entries use semantic action/trigger IDs. Identical copies, identical tokens and multiple triggers cannot be matched by name or guessed by final state. Preserve last-known-source information after death. Existing normal-reset card-ID arrays/mulligan aliases need an explicit binding convention or versioned extension for occurrence-preserving permutations; never pretend v1 already specifies all of this.

The controller consumes actor, decision kind, source, mode, target IDs and ordered roles, payment resources and amounts, additional discard costs, attacks, blocks, damage allocations, trigger ordering, keep/mulligan/bottom, cleanup discard, concession when explicitly tested, and each priority pass. It records **actually consumed** decisions, not a copy of input. Upstream API microsteps may differ; map them into one documented semantic transaction without creating/removing response windows. Deterministic metadata/bookkeeping calls may be allowlisted, but consequential selection must have explicit data. Payment auto-selection, automatic attackers/blockers, target defaults and inherited AI fallbacks are forbidden. The engines independently determine legality and effects.

Implement reset chronology from pinned CR 103.5 and GH-15: declarations in turn order, redraw and explicit ordered bottom choice before that player's next declaration round, rather than a convenient keep-then-bottom approximation. Test zero, one and multiple mulligans, both players mulliganing, duplicate identities and first-player draw skip. A scripted keep can be strategically poor yet legal; replay must not replace it.

Trigger synchronization must distinguish casts from successful resolution, ETB target selection from casting targets, simultaneous controller ordering and APNAP placement, and source death with an ability pending/on stack. Combat scripts specify attackers, blockers and actual damage allocation under the pinned modern rules. There is no obsolete blocker-order decision or response window between ordering blockers and damage. Explicitly test trample/deathtouch, flying/reach, haste/vigilance and multiple blockers. Avoid synthesizing a purported legal combination by reusing old upstream fixtures without vintage review.

## What to compare

Compare at initial/opening decisions; each priority handoff; resolution after applicable state-based actions and trigger placement; completed attack/block declarations; damage plus state-based actions; cleanup including additional cleanup iterations; and game end. A settled cleanup hook is not an extra policy decision. Separate “trigger ordering pending” from “all triggers placed” checkpoints to avoid aligning different semantic moments.

Normalize turn/phase/step, active/priority player, life, six mana values, land-play count, ordered libraries/graveyards and stack, object lineage/generation, owner/controller, tapped/summoning-status basis, damage, power/toughness, keywords, attackers/blockers, targets/modes, temporary effects and duration, and terminal reason/winner. Keep relevant order; canonicalize unordered collections by semantic identity only. Never sort away library/stack order, targets' roles or chosen trigger ordering. Rewards are an mtg-lab API contract derived independently from outcome, not an upstream field presumed to exist.

Each bridge publishes a versioned field/decision observability manifest. A required missing field blocks that test's claimed coverage. Compare legal semantic choices at decision boundaries where reference access permits it. Different internal factoring is normalized to equivalent complete choices/continuations, not compared by candidate index. Selected-action acceptance is weaker than enumeration equivalence: report it separately. Probe legal alternatives and representative invalid actions on isolated replay copies; compare rejection and unchanged state/RNG/decision/private-view snapshots where exported. If references cannot expose their complete legal set or RNG snapshots, mark that component unobservable; retain the mtg-lab rule-based tests, and do not count the weaker bridge check as full equality.

Full-state replay data are privileged. Separately compare authorized views, masks, candidate ordering and error responses under paired hidden-information perturbations. Such pairs assert current observation invariance, not unchanged future draws/outcomes. Test that a policy cannot access opponent hand, library permutation, chance seed or privileged reference snapshots.

## Generation matrix and budgets

Proposed initial dedicated campaign: eight manifest rows × three policy pairings (random/random, heuristic/heuristic, heuristic/coverage-directed with policy seats balanced) × ten independent chance tapes = 240 generation attempts. Use seat-filtered legal choices, record rejected LLM proposals separately, and stop on contract violations. Maintain separate policy RNG from chance generation. Coverage bias targets actual observed interactions, not just rare cards in the deck; use named scenario goals and pinned curated deck orders for hard interactions. Label curated games as directed, not an unbiased random sample.

Start each engine/game at 5 minutes wall time, 10,000 semantic decisions and 200 turns; tune only via reviewed measured receipts. Per-worker memory starts at 4 GiB with one reference JVM worker until profiling supports more. These are safety budgets, not known requirements or performance promises. Theoretical ceiling for 240 games × three engines × five minutes is 60 worker-hours; begin with a 24-game adapter pilot and a two-hour campaign cap, report incompletes, then schedule the full budget explicitly. Cache builds separately; never spend per-game budget compiling. Log CPU/wall time, peak memory, decisions and checkpoint bytes, timeout stage, and all attempted/executed/completed/truncated/failed/unavailable/unsupported/disputed/agreed counts. A timeout is not a rules draw or a killed semantic mutant.

Require the 16-game admission floor plus observed coverage goals (all card behaviors, both Goblin Surprise modes, repeated mana/boost activations, discard costs, trigger ordering, response timing, combat interactions, mulligan/cleanup). If 240 attempts miss a goal, add independently reviewed directed games or focused scenarios; do not secretly lower the goal. Some terminal/interaction cases may require targeted scenarios; do not claim a simultaneous-loss full game is reachable merely because terminal logic supports it. Concession tests remain separate and cannot fill the nonconcession full-game floor.

Development corpus and holdout are separated by authored scenario family/trace ancestry, not just seed. Retire a revealed holdout failure into regression, preserve the original failure and report the resulting loss of holdout independence; replace with independently authored cases in a new frozen version. No headline reliability estimate should ignore adaptive selection or failed attempts.

## Independent oracles and divergence handling

The generator creates stimuli, never expected outcomes. A tape from mtg-lab can inherit missing legal choices or wrong timing; compensate with independently authored scripts, upstream-driven generation after those recording adapters exist, and candidate/rejection comparisons. Mixing generators is diversity, not proof. Agreement of three engines is evidence; pinned rules and card definitions remain authority. Do not majority-vote.

For every discrepancy preserve the earliest checkpoint and last agreed prefix, input pins, consumed tapes and per-field diff. Classify wrong alignment/identity, adapter defect, unsupported observation, rules/card vintage, candidate production defect, upstream defect, or unresolved. Derive the expected behavior from pinned numbered rules/card sources in original prose, with independent review. Fix the relevant adapter/core test-first. Proven upstream exceptions retain failing outputs, rule derivation, upstream issue when available, review and release limitation; they are adjudicated disagreements, not agreements. Missing required adapter coverage cannot become an exception that completes the milestone.

Minimize by replaying prefixes and dependency-aware edits while retaining the exact first-divergence predicate and pins. Remove irrelevant optional actions, simplify selections/permutations, and search earlier branch choices only with replay validation. A full normal-reset minimization must retain both exact 40-card multisets and a legal reachable prefix; deleting a land play may invalidate all later payments. Keep the original terminal game and minimized reachable prefix. A smaller extracted synthetic position is useful as a second fixture but must be labeled synthetic and independently derive expectations. Budget minimization (e.g. 500 candidate replays or 30 minutes), record tried/preserved/rejected candidates, and retain an unminimized failure if exhausted. Do not require reaching the original winner after the first divergence.

## Artifacts, reproducibility and scheduling

Each run records repository commit/dirty state, fixture/schema/card/rules hashes, reference source/card-data/patch/dependency/bridge hashes, toolchain/container/architecture, resolved limits, policy implementation/model/checkpoint and prompt configuration, seed streams, actual chance tape, action transcript, observation capability manifest, consumed choices, checkpoint hashes/full privileged files, outcome accounting, logs, first diff, provenance and adjudication links. Replay compatibility is explicit; model generation itself may not be deterministic, but the frozen accepted transcript must be.

Keep privileged full-state/chance/seed artifacts separate from seat-scoped trajectories and ordinary CI summaries. Do not publish unchecked model prompts/logs, raw Oracle text, rules text, card databases, upstream trees or linked reference binaries. Preserve notices and scoped provenance policy; separate-process execution does not settle Forge licensing. Store large caches/builds outside the checkout and export only reviewed minimal semantic receipts; track completeness/checksums atomically so interruption cannot masquerade as a passing game.

Per-change checks: parser/controller/comparator negative controls, short admitted prefixes, impacted reference cases using cached builds, selected complete tapes once runnable, native replay and privacy tests. Dedicated/nightly campaigns: 240-attempt matrix, expanding directed corpus, minimization, longer fuzzing and mutations. Release: rerun frozen critical and full-game suites against exact candidate with both pinned references; build failure is release failure. Updating CI/workflow gates requires an explicit operations issue; this plan does not authorize policy edits. Existing verification and separate Codex review requirements remain in force.

The practical first deliverable is a three-engine, duplicate-card normal-reset and mulligan replay with strict chance consumption, followed by one complete red/green game. Until that works with rich checkpoints, investing in thousands of AI games would mainly generate unauditable traces.
