Status: **Accepted scope, amended 2026-10-12; delivery still in progress.** Operator-directed
amendment, tracked in [operations #240](https://github.com/pabloxrl/mtg-lab/issues/240).
This is the current delivery plan under [parent #7](https://github.com/pabloxrl/mtg-lab/issues/7).
It supersedes the release scope and sequencing of the earlier
[RFC 0002 program](rfc-0002.md), not its historical evidence or existing regressions.
The pinned RFC and lossless requirement ledger remain unchanged as an audit trail.

The 2026-10-12 operator priority, [operations #318](https://github.com/pabloxrl/mtg-lab/issues/318),
launches the [reference-AI/L0 program](reference-ai-l0.md) using verified M2
interfaces. Capture and real games come first. EV-M3/E1a is not a prerequisite;
its authorized registry/history/drafts remain preserved and its children are
intentionally held. The entire admitted corpus is mandatory fast native L0 after
every change; a curated subset adds deeper instrumentation/live references.
Registration is not corpus delivery or a measured speed claim.

## Goal and boundary

Prove that mtg-lab faithfully supports the frozen red and green toy decks, can
finish unattended games, and can replay games generated by **Forge and XMage's
existing AIs** as a durable torture suite. No learning system is needed.

Keep the [exact decks, card definitions and rules pins](../card-manifests.md):
two 40-card decks, 20 distinct card names and the supported Goblin token. Exercise
RG, GR, RR and GG, each with starting seat 0 and 1: eight manifest rows.
Unsupported cards or bridge choices fail explicitly. Native random/heuristic
players remain useful for engine checks; external AI-generated games provide
additional independent inputs, not a claim that those AIs are correct or strong.

Out of the current scope: RL concepts, reward/learning API expansion, training,
self-play learning, learned-policy evaluation, PettingZoo/Gymnasium and trainer
integrations. Python bindings, training batching, sharded training datasets,
interactive clients and the old hardware throughput qualification are also
deferred unless a later explicit scope amendment brings them back. Reuse existing
recording/replay infrastructure without removing its tests or changing its contracts.
The current milestone has no training prerequisite and makes no learning claim.
Native batching specifically for offline L0 replay is authorized by #318.

## Reusable mechanics, explicit card definitions

The toy decks are a verification boundary, not permission to hard-code rules to
card names. Implement each supported mechanic once. Card definitions provide
costs, characteristics, keywords and ability parameters; any registered creature
with flying must use the same flying/reach blocking rule. A new card composed
entirely of supported mechanics should need a definition and validation tests,
not a new implementation of those mechanics. This is not automatic support for
arbitrary cards, unsupported interactions or a natural-language card-text interpreter.

Current code is a hybrid: [combat](../../crates/mtg-core/src/combat.rs) shares
keyword rules through [definitions](../../crates/mtg-core/src/card_definitions.rs),
while [trigger kinds](../../crates/mtg-core/src/triggers.rs) still include
card-specific Archer, Cyclops and Pyromancer variants. Do not claim a fully generic
ability system. The [data-driven card design](../rfcs/0003-data-driven-cards.md) selects a runtime
catalog, typed declarative abilities and shared rules. Registration #241 split its migration into bounded deliveries under EV-M3.
That architecture remains required before future card-pool expansion, but is
not a prerequisite for current-pool reference capture or corpus qualification.
Prove reusable behavior across distinct definitions/characteristics and relevant
ability combinations, with focused tests as well as the frozen-deck games. Keep
current tests and serialization/replay compatibility explicit during any refactor;
new mechanic support still requires its own rules-derived implementation and tests.

Counts are floors, not correctness proofs. The ten-game pilots may be included
in later frozen corpora only when they meet the final admission contract; report
overlap explicitly. A 100-attempt benchmark does not imply 100 successful games.
Failed or truncated attempts never count toward the 1,000 complete-game floor.
Repeated replays of one game are not distinct games; retain content identities
and report duplicate generation attempts. Add attempts if duplicates or failures
prevent meeting the source/matchup quotas. Natural rules draws count as complete;
concessions and safety-limit terminations do not fill the normal-game quota.

## Capture and replay contract

Capture more than a readable game log. Each artifact pins the source engine,
bridge, AI configuration, decks/card/rules revisions, seat assignments and seeds.
Record actual ordered shuffles and mulligans, every consequential choice (including
passes, payments, targets, trigger ordering, attacks, blocks and damage choices),
physical card/token identity, intermediate semantic checkpoints and final outcome.
The record must support replay without invoking the AI again. Equal seed numbers
across engines do not imply equal games; inject the recorded chance outcomes and
choices through strict controllers while each engine computes the rules itself.

Compare at aligned semantic boundaries: opening decisions, priority changes,
spell/ability resolution and state-based actions, combat, cleanup and termination.
Compare the fields needed to validate each interaction, including life, zones and
order, stack, mana, object characteristics, targets, effects, turn and actor.
Missing/extra choices, unconsumed randomness, identity ambiguity and unsupported
callbacks fail visibly. Never overwrite derived game state to manufacture agreement.
Full-state traces are privileged; keep them separate from player-visible inputs.
Use the detailed [replay design](../testing/game-replay.md) for technical contracts;
this amendment controls its superseded campaign sizes and delivery order.

The source engine supplies candidate evidence, not a golden answer. Preserve the
first differing checkpoint and classify adapter errors, card/rules-version
mismatches, mtg-lab defects and upstream defects against pinned rules and card
sources. Independently review adjudications. An explained upstream defect stays
visible as an exception, not an agreement; retain the original trace and a
rules-derived regression. Missing required bridge support cannot be waived as an
exception. Final qualification requires all discrepancies resolved or explicitly
adjudicated with their limitations recorded, never silently accepted output.

## Torture-suite composition and speed

Generate games occasionally, freeze their transcripts, then replay without AI
search in routine verification. Keep the original full games alongside minimized
regressions and focused positive, negative, interaction, privacy and recording
failure tests. Random seeds cannot guarantee rare-case coverage. Retain the
existing M2 scenario floor, capability assertions and the independently authored
dual-reference critical suite (at least 20 scenarios); generated volume replaces none.

Replay the entire admitted corpus as mandatory offline native L0 after every
change, plus the existing regression foundation. Use a prebuilt release runner,
local immutable artifacts, full required semantic assertions and fail-closed
manifest/checksum validation. No reference AI/JVM runs in ordinary L0. Optional
subsets provide feedback only; a deterministic curated deeper subset adds richer
invariants and live references. [Operations #332](https://github.com/pabloxrl/mtg-lab/issues/332)
owns reviewed CI wiring/scheduling and measured latency budget/thresholds; existing
checks stay mandatory until equivalent-or-stronger policy passes review. Measure
cold process start/load/checksums/validation/full replay/comparison separately from
builds, warm throughput and reference generation. The initial 1,000-game purpose
is seconds to low minutes, not an unmeasured speed promise; report/optimize gaps
without reducing assertions or quotas.
Keep a separately frozen, independently authored holdout; record when a discovered
failure is moved into the development regression set.

Generation estimates are currently **unmeasured**. Publish observed total elapsed
time and successful games per hour alongside per-game latency and resource use.
Include conversion and recording overhead, failures/retries and achieved worker
concurrency in the 1,000-game projection. Never infer reference-AI speed from a
scripted mtg-lab replay or treat a safety timeout as a benchmark result.

The existing [program manifest](rfc-0002.json) authorizes only M0–M2. M2/E1 has
verified completion in the [gate report](../evidence/m2-gate/README.md) and linked
protected-merge/exact-main receipts. No legacy successor is authorized. Legacy M3–M5
and planning operations #81–#83 are closed as not planned under the operator's tracker cleanup, not completed. Their

they are not acceptance prerequisites for this new engine-validation milestone.

launches only the new **Engine validation M3** under the 2026-10-12 operator

[contracts and handoff](engine-validation-m3.md), and GitHub milestone 7 cannot
resume legacy M3. Its protected registration and first activation are historical
delivery, not M3 completion. The 2026-10-12 priority hold now prevents dispatch of

selection. Feature workers cannot expand the registry. The separate [L0 registry](reference-ai-l0.json), registered by #318, now owns
E2–E5 capture/corpus work. It uses M2 directly, never waits for M3, and launches
only after its protected registration and exact-main CI. M3's intentional holds
are preserved; neither L0 registration nor completion automatically resumes it.
No new cards, RL, broad engine rewrite or general scripting VM is authorized.

No current stage is complete because this document exists. Keep evidence on the
parent workpad and capability reference. Completing E5 completes this amended
engine-validation scope, not the deferred original RFC or a general Magic engine.

The [continuous performance contract](reference-ai-l0.md#continuous-matched-cross-engine-performance-monitoring)
registers matched same-game native/XMage/Forge benchmarks, durable timing/ratio
history and reviewed scheduled regression monitoring. Native full-corpus timing
is recorded on each required L0 run; costly three-engine monitoring runs separately
nightly when changes land, on adapter/corpus changes and on demand. Fixed cohort
trends and full-corpus release comparisons retain raw timings, failures, hardware
and instrumentation limits. This is planned delivery, not measured speed or
operational monitoring evidence; capture remains first.
